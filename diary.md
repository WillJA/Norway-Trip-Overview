\outl0\strokewidth0 \strokec2 **Day 1 - 4th July 2026**\
Got a 05:20 Uber to Bristol bus station for the 06:00 coach to Heathrow. It was uneventful, apart from Lucy getting severely travel sick right at the end and having to swallow down several mouthfuls of bile.\
\
The flight was smooth. We took a train to Oslo from the airport and checked into the hotel. We had a mooch around and a very underwhelming burger. We mainly enjoyed the (admittedly very impressive) architecture.\
\
Then we used one of the eight condoms that Lucy very optimistically packed.\
\
**Day 2 - 5th July 2026**\
A lazy morning, followed by a lovely coffee and pastry (I even treated myself to a flat white) before nosing around some very cool apartment buildings with steps leading right into the canal (though the water looked a bit off-putting, to be honest).\
\
Then we had a surprisingly smooth four-hour bus journey from Oslo to Gjendbu for the start of the hike. No travel sickness and excellent views for the majority of the trip.\
\
We then embarked on a 2km hike to reach a superb (if slightly windy) campsite by the lake. It was very quiet indeed\'97an excellent spot to begin!\
\
**Day 3 - 6th July 2026**\
A 10-hour sleep and an 08:00 wake-up to start the day\'97very relaxed! We had our first of many portions of porridge before breaking camp and setting off at the quite late time of 10:00-ish.\
\
The hike quickly turned into a bit of an epic, with the Bessegen ridge proving to be a beautiful if slightly tricky adversary. The scramble section on the way down with our heavy packs made it a little spicy. We only managed 17km but had to work hard for it; it was all worth it for the views of the lake and the mountain panorama, though.\
\
We descended to Memurubu with tired legs and stopped at a great camp spot about 1km and 300m above the hut.\
\
**Day 4 - 7th July 2026**\
The day of the chain.\
\
Had a lovely, civilised poo in the Memurubu hut before embarking on the climb up the next ridge; there were excellent waterfall views to be had. Then we began a very, very steep descent down to the lake, which involved plenty of chain abseiling and concurrent shouts of \'93You\'92re doing great, Lucy!\'94 Then, a short but mercifully flat section around the lake to get to the next hut. We then had 4km on a very gradual uphill and, after a bit of faff, found another absolute peach of a camp spot overlooking the lake\'97we even had a rainbow for company.\
\
**Day 5 - 8th July 2026**\
A gradual climb up over a pass before a sharp (but not as sharp as yesterday) descent to Fondsbu, where we indulged in a long lunch and an extended planning session. We did a lot of route editing here to include some self-service huts en route\'97an excellent choice, as it turns out.\
\
Then we encountered our first (of many, it turns out) extended boulder field sections. This made for an extremely slow and difficult afternoon as we hopped from boulder to boulder, with serious worries about whether we\'92d manage to find a moderately suitable campsite.\
\
**Day 6 - 9th July 2026**\
The day that started in the boulders and ended by an idyllic river. We emerged from our slightly exposed camp spot to another dry tent but with a chill in the air. It was another fairly relaxed morning, with the main source of stress coming from two people emerging from over a ridge 50m away while I was cleaning up after my morning poo; I\'92ve never wiped so fast.\
\
Then, a very technical start to the hike down some more boulder fields before they relented and we descended a beautiful valley on a fairly gradual decline. At this point, Lucy almost stepped on some ptarmigan chicks that had separated from their mother across the path.\
\
After this gradual descent, we reached a fairly busy path down to the Villamorke self-serve hut\'97a steep 300m down just to reload with food (many Mellom bars, etc.) and hike straight back up. We found a beautiful camp spot right on the riverbank with a flat spot just (and only just) big enough for our tent. Then, a relaxed evening and a much-needed clean in the river (see my earlier comment on the morning\'92s poo).\
\
**Day 7 - 10th July 2026**\
Fannar\'e5ki summit day!\
\
The day started with a shortish climb and then a descent to the Skogadalsb\'f8en hut, where we found all the workers having an idyllic lunch break, swimming in the river, and generally loving life. On the way, we saw a father and son duo; the father was 87 years old\'97very impressive!\
\
We then hatched a plan to camp at the foot of Fannar\'e5ki on the SE side and do an out-and-back to the summit that evening. Off we went up a gradual and quite well-maintained climb to our \'93basecamp.\'94 Camp spots were hard to come by, so we pitched up at around 1400m by a lake, about 50m or so from a private cabin (there was no evidence of people when we pitched).\
\
Then, a wonderful out-and-back to the summit with one light bag between us. There were a surprising number of snowfields to cross and some fairly technical paths, but nothing crazy. The views at the top were spectacular; we were very lucky to have perfect visibility. God knows where people camp at the summit\'97it\'92s just one massive boulder field.\
\
A lovely descent, feeling pretty pleased with ourselves, was tainted only by the realisation that the previously uninhabited cabin was now showing signs of life. We didn\'92t have any issues, though, and the dinner we had was the most well-earned of the trip yet\'97a superb day all round.\
\
**Day 8 - 11th July 2026**\
A day of two halves! We had a leisurely start after our summit heroics the night before and were greeted by a very chilled beginning to the hike. A gentle descent, with the second half on a gravel road, made for some easy morning mileage.\
\
We then found that the route crossed a main road with a 4-star hotel conveniently placed. We both got a smash burger, which was just what the doctor ordered. Another slightly too luxuriously long lunch stop meant we were late leaving but very full and content.\
\
The climb out from the hotel was fairly intense with a very steep start. When we reached the top, we had a cordial chat with a Norwegian guy who had his fishing rod with him but hadn\'92t caught a fish in 10 days of trying.\
\
We then descended into mosquito country. A pretty steep trail down culminated in a very steep river gorge and some major issues trying to find a campsite at the bottom. We had to carry on up a pretty punchy climb while being plagued by mozzies. In the end, we pitched up close to the next self-serve hut on a mediocre site at around 20:30\'97so many mozzies that it was an "eat in the tent" situation.\
\
**Day 9 - 12th July 2026**\
Woke up to a scene of hundreds of mozzies caught between the outer and flysheet, expecting the worst. I put on full waterproofs before getting out\'97then no mozzies at all outside!! So, an unexpected win to start.\
\
Then we started on what we knew would be an intense day: up and over a big climb with a glacier crossing at the top. The terrain was indeed bleak and technical, with the worst of it after descending slightly on the other side. Lots of snow crossings with big, big boulders in between made for a slow but very interesting day.\
\
The glacier crossing itself was easy, just like the rest of the snow we\'92d encountered before, albeit for longer sections between rocks.\
\
After what felt like an eternal descent on big old rocks, the gradient relented and the last few kilometres to the next hut was a gentle hike down the valley. We then pushed on another 4km or so from the hut to find a campsite by a promising-looking lake, cutting down on the distance we had to do to reach our endpoint the next day.\
\
After a frustrating diversion up and over a hillside due to \'93blasting operations,\'94 we reached a nice (if very silty) camp spot and enjoyed our last night in the wilds!! As we were eating dinner at around 19:30, we saw a very, very weary couple descending the path\'97still 4km from the hut. The girl, in particular, looked absolutely finished and was moving very slowly\'85 a big day out for them indeed.\
\
**Day 10 - 13th July 2026**\
The final hike day! We\'92d read a lot about this being a tough day with slow-going terrain, so there was a bit of apprehension. This day is effectively the first stage of the Massiv trail but in reverse, so there\'92s been a lot written on blogs, etc., talking about the unrelenting boulder fields.\
\
The day started with a surprisingly pleasant climb up to the lake where the boulders began, but the terrain was no worse than what we\'92ve seen previously and certainly easier than the day before. After skirting the lake, the boulders relented slightly before coming back with a vengeance up and over the final summit. We descended the other side and eventually exited the boulder field onto a steepish, rocky trail down to the DNT hut at Sota S\'e6ter, which marks the endpoint of the hike!\
\
It was surprisingly difficult to find a good camp spot (we wild camped and just paid for a shower and breakfast), but we got a flattish pitch by the river, which looked inviting but was, in fact, far too cold for any prolonged bathing. The shower at the campsite was bliss\'97the longest and most needed shower either of us has ever had. I felt like a new man!\
